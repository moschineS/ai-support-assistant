## Immediate actions (first minutes)

1. Block first, investigate second: block the affected card and/or online banking access while still on the call. Blocking is free and reversible; a delayed block is not.
2. Identify the fraud pattern: phishing link, fake support call ("bank employee" asking for TANs), marketplace scam, or unauthorized card usage. The pattern decides the follow-up path.
3. Document verbatim what the customer clicked, told, or approved — including approved pushTAN prompts, which matter for the liability assessment.

## Follow-up path

Unauthorized card transactions go to the chargeback procedure with provisional re-credit within 1 business day. Authorized-but-deceived payments (customer transferred money themselves) get a recall attempt to the recipient bank immediately — speed matters more than completeness of the report; the recall can be filed with minimal data and completed later.

## What agents must never say

Never assign blame ("you should not have clicked that") and never prejudge liability — the liability assessment is a separate back-office decision based on gross negligence criteria. Always recommend a police report for completed fraud; provide the written confirmation the police will ask for.
